# Scope and limits

Circulation mode calls BoreTHM's coupled bore and annulus temperature calculation. It retains the model's hydraulics, liquid-property guards and radial rock response. The exported pressure profiles distinguish absolute pressure from gauge pressure. Prescribed annulus heating is integrated across its declared depth knots. A sweep repeats a declared set of circulation inputs, with at most 25 cases; it does not optimise an operating programme or estimate a probability distribution.

Site assessment calls the model's evaluator with a frozen site definition. The evaluator selects its programme and reports stability windows and equipment qualifications. The application preserves these status decisions. With a BoreTHM that accepts operating choices, the Programme page can state the hydraulic programme and any of those choices, and the evaluator uses them; otherwise it selects every one itself.

A survey defines measured depth and vertical depth, with positions where sufficient directional data exist. Temperature and hydraulic calculations support paths whose vertical depth does not decrease. Survey reconstruction can display other paths, whose calculations are unavailable. Deviated stability assessment is unavailable.

Wall histories solve independent radial columns under prescribed circulating or static boundaries. They omit axial rock conduction and feedback to a circulating well. Tool histories use a prescribed surrounding temperature column with lumped steel and fluid heat capacities. They omit coolant transport delay and feedback to that column. Scheduled wall interventions support a fixed radial domain. Checkpoints record full physical state at completed event boundaries. Display frames alone cannot restart an integration.

Each scenario selects a named set of physical assumptions. New scenarios use `physics="v1.8.0"`: the model uses its Herschel-Bulkley annulus treatment with the Erge flow-transition band, Colebrook pipe friction and zero wall roughness. Herschel-Bulkley describes fluid shear stress as a yield stress plus a power of the shear rate. Colebrook relates pipe resistance to the flow and wall roughness. `physics="v1.7.0"` restores the previous annulus treatment and Blasius pipe friction. The installed model reports the settings used; manifests and exports record them.

The high-temperature preset is a water case with a formation at 450 °C at 3,000 m. Under current physics (`v1.8.0`) the model's circulation solve reports no success; its unsuccessful profile reaches about 1,500 °C. This is a non-convergence, and the inputs are inside the water-property table. The application returns `SOLVER_NOT_CONVERGED` with the message "The circulation calculation did not converge for these inputs." and publishes no profile. A result the model reports as unsuccessful ends the run without further initialisation attempts. The same preset converges with a bit bore delivery temperature of about 221 °C with friction heating off, and of about 229.6 °C under previous physics (`v1.7.0`), which uses Blasius pipe friction. A direct model call with current physics and Blasius pipe friction converges at about 226 °C. Similar water cases fail to converge at some flows and formation temperatures under either physics choice, without a simple pattern.

The water-property table (1–479 °C, 0.1–218.1 MPa absolute) limits the fluid's states. It does not limit the rock: a formation hotter than 479 °C is accepted, and the run goes to the solver. A vertical water well with 500 °C rock at 3,000 m, a 20 °C inlet and 30 kg/s converges with the fluid below 255 °C under either physics choice; the same well at 60 kg/s does not converge. Declared inlet temperatures and surface pressures outside the table return `INPUT_DOMAIN_INVALID` before solving. `PROPERTY_DOMAIN_OUTSIDE` applies to a converged fluid profile outside the table.

Schema 1.0 scenarios load as schema 1.1 with `physics="v1.7.0"`. A visible qualification explains that choice. New scenarios use schema 1.1. Existing results retain their recorded environment requirements. Archives and checkpoints made with BoreTHM 1.6.0 cannot be reproduced; their scenarios can be re-run with previous physics on the current model.

Prescribed thermal histories do not solve hydraulic equations. Their records identify the selected physics and state that its hydraulic settings are unused.

Histories and checkpoints use the observations provided by BoreTHM 1.8.0. The service reports the installed version and checks its function signatures.

Reproduction compares installed model and application versions, the model commit for a Git installation, and installed versions of dependencies named by the lock. It also compares the saved request. Property-array fingerprint differences are reported; they do not by themselves reject reproduction across platforms. The numerical checks still apply. Checkpoint continuation requires matching build fields and the saved radial grid, with the model's eight-unit limit on differences in the last representable place.

Circulation reproduction requires identical saved coordinates and component identifiers. Temperature tolerance is 0.001 degrees Celsius. Pressure tolerance is the greater of 1 Pa and one millionth of the reference magnitude. Other scalar tolerance is 0.001. Assessment comparisons use the model reference projection: 0.002 specific gravity, 0.1 degrees Celsius, 10,000 Pa, 100 m for the field named `md`, 0.1 m for `target_m`, and 1e-8 for remaining numerical fields. Status and programme identifiers must match.

History comparisons retain their declared physical units. Temperature tolerance is 0.001 degrees Celsius. Energy tolerance is the greater of 1 J per metre and one millionth of the reference magnitude. Time tolerance is the greater of 1e-8 seconds and 1e-9 times the reference magnitude. Wall-flux tolerance is the greater of 0.001 W per metre and 1e-9 times the reference magnitude. Coordinates and declared physical inputs must match exactly. Unknown numerical fields fail the comparison. A failed physical balance remains a failure even when repeated values agree. The radial grid may be reused within the model's representable-value bound. No tolerance is enlarged to accommodate a platform difference.

The CI workflow tests Windows and Linux with Python 3.11 and 3.14. Local execution evidence is reported separately from the source tree. Internal trial-state property lookups are not comprehensively instrumented. Use the model's documented assumptions and the returned qualifications when interpreting a result.

A joint declares its kind. An omitted transition lets the selected physics decide: current physics uses an 11.5-degree included bore taper for ordinary drill-pipe joints; other kinds use abrupt transitions. An explicit tapered joint requires its angle. The annulus treatment remains abrupt. Gradual transitions omit the geometric local loss.

A segment can declare one tool class and its collar size in inches. A named tool uses the model's generic allowance under current physics; previous physics applies no generic allowance. These values describe a class and size band. Enter the maker's figure when known. A supplied ToolLoss overrides the generic allowance. The declared loss basis and segment span determine whether it adds to passage loss or replaces internal loss across that span. A zero allowance with no source remains qualified.

The bounded initialisation policy under current physics repeats a successful target solve once, using its accepted temperature profile as the initial estimate. Each attempt records the model solve count. This refinement addresses the observed warm-start difference against the existing temperature tolerance. Previous physics retains the preceding numerical policy. The deterministic policy remains a single target solve.

## Units

Entry and display use SI or oilfield units. Scenarios, archives, contracts and the model hold SI values. The unit choice is an application setting stored in `settings.json` in the data directory; it is not part of a scenario or its hash. `src/borethm_workbench/units.py` holds the table and factors: 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 in = 0.0254 m, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 US gal = 3.785411784 L, 1 psi = 6,894.757293168 Pa and 1 ppg = 119.826427 kg/m³. Specific gravity uses the model's reference of 1,000 kg/m³. Thermal conductivity uses the International Table British thermal unit, and power uses mechanical horsepower. An entered value becomes an SI value to 12 significant figures, so the same quantity entered in either system gives the same scenario. Mass flow and volume flow keep the kind the scenario declares. Run archives keep SI values; the data table's CSV download uses the chosen units.

## Site editor

The site editor holds a site as the keys and SI values of a BoreTHM 1.8.0 site file. On every change the service writes the TOML, loads it with the model's `load_site` and checks it with `validate_site`. Errors and warnings appear beside the field they name; a message the editor cannot place appears above the form. The evidence tier and the plotted profiles come from the loaded site. A site file's hydraulic programme is kept unchanged and is not edited in the form. Writing a built-in site to TOML and loading it reproduces the site loaded from the packaged file field by field. The packaged Soultz file records two provenance entries that the model's built-in definition lacks, and the packaged Newberry stress fits differ from the built-in ones in their last digits.

## Results pages and reports

The assessment results page shows values from the evaluator result, through a unit conversion. With a BoreTHM that returns the result keys described under Results of an assessment, the widths, margins, pass flags, pump limit and the bounds against depth are the model's own. With BoreTHM 1.8.0 the page charts each state and case from the bounds and binding depths the model returns, and states that the widths, margins and pump limit are not returned; the application does not compute them. The verdict, controlling constraint and summary lines are the evaluator's own text, labelled as such; their units are SI whatever the unit choice. Qualifications appear as plain sentences on the results page and in the report; the codes appear in the details views.

The report is one HTML page generated from the saved run: its manifest and creation date. It contains no generation time and loads no external files. It prints from a browser to A4 or Letter.

## Field comparisons

The Field comparisons page and the tested-range check use data files built by `deploy/build_evidence.py` from BoreTHM at the `v1.8.0` tag: the validation guide's FORGE 16B whole-well and FORGE 16A blind circulation comparisons, and the tables those sections cite. `sources.json` records each source path, the tag, its commit and each checksum. The comparisons cover circulating conditions only; no run-in, trip or connection temperatures were compared.

The tested range covers bit depth, bottom-hole formation temperature, volume flow, mass flow, inlet temperature, fluid type and pipe type. The 16A tables record formation temperature and volume flow; the 16B tables record volume flow and the modelled mass rate. A string counts as insulated when any of its pipe layers has a thermal conductivity below 10 W/(m K). For a circulation run the check uses the scenario inputs and the formation temperature at the bit from the result. For an assessment it uses the target depth and temperature, the pump inlet temperature, and the pipe, flow and fluid the evaluator used. The check lists each input outside the range and never blocks a run.

## Programme and operating choices

The Programme page is available when the installed BoreTHM provides the settled operations interface: `Operations` with mass and volume flow fields, `operation_defaults`, `validate_operations` with field records, and `evaluate`'s `operations` argument. The service reports this as `programme_interface` in `/api/v1/capabilities`. With BoreTHM 1.8.0, or a BoreTHM with an earlier form of the interface, the page and the Design levers page are hidden with a note, and every other feature works as before.

The hydraulic programme is the site file's `[programme]` table, the model's reference programme with stated options, or a custom string with optional hole and casing intervals. A custom string's section lengths must add up to the target depth; the model checks this. A section's pipe is one of the model's named pipes, whose wall layers come from the installed model, or user layers with their conductivities. The programme is stored in the site file's `[programme]` table, which the model reads. A section with one of the model's named pipes is written with its `pipe` key, and a section with one wall layer with its diameters and conductivity. Two kinds of section cannot be written there: a user pipe of more than one wall layer, and a named tool with a stated basis or span, as the reference assembly's MWD pulser has (the table's tools entries take neither). A programme with such a section is stored in the scenario as `definition.programme` and passed to the evaluator as a `HydraulicProgramme`, and the page says why. `definition.programme` is left out of the scenario when absent, so a scenario without one keeps the input hash it had before.

Operating choices are written to the site file's `[operations]` table, which the model reads and validates. Each row shows the default value, the rule and its source as the model's `operation_defaults` gives them; the application reads none of the model's internal constants, and a test checks this. A stated value has a basis and a source, as site inputs do. The model's validation records name the field each message belongs to, and the message appears beside that row. A stated value is used as given: where the model would otherwise search, such as for the flow, it does not look for another value, and the result reports any check the value fails. A site file without the two tables gives the assessment it gave before.

A flow is stated as a mass flow or as a volume flow at surface, at the fluid's density at 20 °C, and is entered and shown in kg/s or L/s, or in lb/s or US gal/min with oilfield units. The same applies to the grid of flows the model searches.

## Results of an assessment

Window widths, the bounds at each evaluated depth with the mechanism that sets each, the pump limit and margins, and the tool and coating margins and pass flags are the model's result keys. The application does not compute any of them. A result from BoreTHM 1.8.0 lacks these keys; its results page shows the bounds and peaks the model returned and states that the rest is not returned. Rows the model marks with `sets_status` false are labelled as reported for comparison; this is the unstaged run-in. The pump flows are the model's L/s values. Stated values are listed with their basis and source, and the page says whether one of them is used in the check behind the controlling constraint, in the model's wording.

## Design levers

The Design levers page starts from an assessment run in the application. It changes one input at a time, with 2 to 5 values each, or two inputs as a grid, with at most 25 cases. The inputs are the flow, as a mass or volume flow, the pump inlet temperature, the pipe type, the length of the top string section (for a custom string of two or more sections, where the next section takes up the difference), the drilling fluid density, the 90th-percentile connection duration, the run-in staging interval and minutes, and the tool limit. Every other input stays at the base run's value. Each case is a complete assessment queued as an ordinary run, one at a time, and opens and exports on its own. The estimate before starting is the base run's duration times the number of cases. Cancelling stops the running case and the queued ones; finished cases are kept.

The table shows each case's verdict, controlling constraint, tool margin, window widths and pump margin from its saved result. The tool margin is the smallest of the drilling, connection and staged run-in margins; the pump margin is the smaller of drilling and the trip. The tornado chart shows, for each lever, the smallest and largest change of each margin from the base run: a change is a case's returned value minus the base run's. Each bar is labelled with the lowest and highest computed value of its lever, in the chosen units. The grid shows each case's verdict and controlling constraint. These are computed cases. They are not an optimisation, imply nothing between the computed values and give no probability. A study exports as one archive, with each case's run archive, and as one HTML report.
